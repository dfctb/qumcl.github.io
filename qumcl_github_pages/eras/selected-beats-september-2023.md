# Selected Beats (September 2023)

The compilation Selected Beats (released September 2023) is another fundamental milestone in Gerasenko's discography. However, its concept is different: it is not ambient, but an anthology of rhythmic patterns, breakbeats, and raw IDM accumulated during his experiments. The music here is entirely focused on groove, broken structures, and hypnotic beats — pure, harsh IDM mixed with ambient, experimental electronics, and techno.

The # Album (September 2023)

The album # (released September 27, 2023, also known as "by syringeee with love" ) features a signature dense lo-fi IDM and weirdcore sound with a deep immersion into gaming nostalgia and a raw cybernetic atmosphere. While previous releases were dark or harsh, this album sounds like a hacker thriller from the early 2000s, where every track is a digital system breach. The sound is rough but simultaneously groovy and rhythmic.

Track breakdown:

1.    "Eat Like Him" (04:58) — an intriguing opener that begins with a viscous atmosphere and gradually develops into broken, sluggish IDM rhythm, creating a psychedelic effect.

2.    "System Shock" (04:33) — a direct reference to the cult sci-fi game System Shock and the AI SHODAN; aggressive glitches, grinding synths, and a broken cyber-industrial beat simulate a computer system crash.

3.    "Has To Be The Way We Like It" (03:21) — a more dynamic and compact track with broken IDM rhythms featuring a sample from a 1960s Pink Floyd interview.

4.    "Deus Ex" (03:39) — another gaming homage, this time to the conspiracy cyberpunk of Deus Ex, with a very fast rhythm, spy thriller atmosphere, neon night cities, and dark technologies.

5.    "Transit System" (07:36) — the longest and most monumental track, a hypnotic seven-and-a-half-minute journey with a monotonous beat like a train moving through darkness, featuring a sample from the Black Mesa remake.

6.    "Riddim Menace" (02:57) — a short, angry, and aggressive track where breakbeats become ragged and prickly, creating a sense of danger.

7.    "Acid Teddy" (05:26) — an experiment with classic acid sound, mixing characteristic acid synth modulations with raw lo-fi style.

8.    "Who Wants To Work Today?" (02:33) — a mini-track built around a sample from an old Soviet film, turning everyday micro-sounds into a glitched rhythm that ends as abruptly as it begins.

9.    "Syd Barrett" (03:15) — the final dedication to Syd Barrett (founder of Pink Floyd, whose solo album The Madcap Laughs gave Gerasenko his pseudonym), a melancholic, psychedelic closing point.

This release represents an ideal balance between cyberpunk aesthetics (System Shock, Deus Ex) and Gerasenko's personal history (the Syd Barrett tribute). The music sounds cohesive, like a single soundtrack to an old sci-fi film recorded on a worn VHS tape.

I AM ABSOLUTELY SURE THERE ARE NO CORPSES IN OUR KITCHEN (December 2023)

During the September period, Gerasenko recorded the most important album in his discography — one that changed his sound from early overloaded chaos to a more cinematic, ambient, minimalist approach using a completely different music production method.

The album I AM ABSOLUTELY SURE THERE ARE NO CORPSES IN OUR KITCHEN (released December 2023) is a fundamental turning point in Gerasenko's entire body of work. This release divides his discography into "before" and "after" , radically transforming the dirty, overloaded chaos of early recordings into mature, cinematic minimalism.

*Concept: a sonic imprint of schizophrenia. * This is a deeply personal, therapeutic, and unsettling work. Gerasenko has stated directly that the album is based on his real schizophrenic experience and paranoia. The conceptual inspiration came from the cult psychological text game Milk outside a bag of milk outside a bag of milk... , in which a simple trip to the kitchen or a shop turns into a psychedelic nightmare. The music is no longer merely a collection of tracks; it is structured as a *single 25-part audio diary* (from waking up to panic attack and taking medication).

*Key changes in sound (new approach). * Cinematic minimalism: instead of a dense wall of sound and aggressive broken rhythms, Gerasenko leaves much "air" . The sound becomes transparent, fragile, and unsettlingly realistic. Music works precisely: one melancholic synth passage or a distant rustle frightens more than tons of overloaded distortion. Everyday concrete music: the sound is filled with tangible field recordings, creating a chilling effect of presence. Subdued, muffled lo-fi: signature tape hiss is now used not for chaos but for the effect of "isolation" . The music sounds as if heard through water or through a locked bedroom door.

*Chronicle of sonic psychosis (album structure breakdown). * The album reproduces the phases of a mental crisis in detail.

1.    Routine and rising paranoia — the short ambient opener "Wake Up" transitions into deceptively calm, melancholic "Good Morning" and everyday "Breakfast". But already in "Thinking In My Bed", the music slows down, immersing the listener in a viscous, sticky anticipation of disaster.

2.    Peak of madness — on "The First Attack Of Schizophrenia" and "HALLUCINATIONS", minimalism gives way to sharp sonic artefacts, glitches, and disorienting frequencies. "VOICES" literally materialises auditory hallucinations through distorted audio fragments.

3.    Chemical lull — on "I'M TAKING PILLS" and "I Feel The Effects Of The Pills", the rhythm completely dissolves. The sound becomes static, cottony, and sluggish. Withdrawal into oneself follows ("After The Pills, I Went Into Myself" ). The album's finale, through "I Got Paranoid" and "I Turned On The Music To Get Rid Of The Hallucinations", loops the nightmare, showing that for the author, music is the only way to escape the madness.

This album is a powerful art object. Gerasenko stopped simply writing "electronic beats" and created a tangible, eerie, yet incredibly beautiful soundtrack to human loneliness and the struggle with one's own mind.

Hospitalisation and Weirdrum Records

Unfortunately, during the period of recording this album, Gerasenko experienced several suicide attempts, leading to his early hospitalisation in a psychiatric facility from late September to early December. During his hospital stay, he was diagnosed with paranoid schizophrenia. He released the first part only after leaving the hospital on Weirdrum Records.

Regarding Weirdrum Records: Gerasenko joined the label by invitation in September 2023 and remained a member until autumn 2024, leaving due to a misunderstanding between the management and himself. He demanded that all his releases be removed from the label, though the label still lists some of his releases among its own.
