# July 7--8: Raw Experiments and Archival Dumps (Madcap Laughs)

Clunky Bug (July 7, Madcap Laughs): A musical microcosm frozen in an isolated Siberian room. Hypnotic melodies layer over fast, crumbly jungle rhythms, creating psychological dissonance. Aggression here does not conflict with fragility — they coexist in a single weirdcore space, turning the narrow theme of "insect life" into a universal experience of digital paranoia.

Funny Punny (July 8, Madcap Laughs): A digital explosion of absurd joy born from deep boredom — deliberate unseriousness where strange sounds and playful motifs collide with fast rhythms. Even noise elements do not threaten; they only reinforce the comedic atmosphere, as if the computer itself is amusing itself.

Qualopec (Compilation Unreleased Tracks) (July 8, Madcap Laughs): The first collection of unreleased experiments from summer 2023 to spring 2024. Not merely a sketchbook, but a sonic portrait of an artist on the verge of self-discovery, where experimental IDM refuses purity in favour of raw sincerity.

Lost Works (July 8, Madcap Laughs): A digital archive of a lost era where retrofuturism collides with gritty reality. Micro-sounds and glitches imitate the internal screams of dying equipment — from disk drives to the blue screen of death.

H₃O+OH⇌H₂O+H₂O (July 21, Madcap Laughs): A conceptual mini-album built around the chemistry of acids and pharmaceutical delirium. The *8-minute track "Benzhexol"* references the strong hallucinogenic drug trihexyphenidyl, which induces severe deliriant hallucinations. The sound is viscous and oppressive, with sudden noise attacks simulating mental blackouts — the opposite of psychedelic expansion, continuing the line of his earlier "schizophrenic" albums.

By summer 2024, Gerasenko shifted dramatically from the intimate domestic horror of the Kitchen diptych toward large-scale, cinematic mythological imagery. The four albums released under his main qumcl alias in July 2024 form an interconnected "Mythological Tetralogy" — a cohesive sonic journey exploring ancient Greece, Egypt, and the liminal spaces between.

This cycle represents a quantum leap in his production quality, compositional ambition, and conceptual depth. It solidified qumcl as a mature, visionary project capable of creating vast, immersive sound worlds.

The Tetralogy's Structure and Narrative Arc:

The four albums are best understood as a single, unfolding cinematic experience:

1.    From Atmos (July 5) — The Cosmic Prologue

2.    To Athene (July 6) — The Ruins of Greece

3.    Egyptology (July 14) — The Tombs of Egypt

4.    Olympians (July 26) — The Power of Olympus

Together, they move from the cold emptiness of space to the crumbling grandeur of ancient civilisations, ending in a cathartic, explosive celebration of mythic power.

1. From Atmos (July 5, 2024) — The Cosmic Prologue

From Atmos acts as the overture to the entire cycle. Its title, a play on "atmosphere" and "atoms," immediately establishes a sense of scale — cosmic, elemental, and vast.

The album is not set in any specific historical period but in a liminal, pre-creation void. It serves as a "cosmic bridge" between the earlier, claustrophobic cybernetic decay and the ancient worlds to come. The listener is suspended in space, observing abandoned orbital infrastructure and the silent birth of myth.

Deep, cold, and submerged drones dominate. Rhythms are present but buried, acting more like distant pulsars than danceable beats. The tape hiss and lo-fi textures are not a sign of decay here but of distance — as if the music is a faint transmission from a forgotten galaxy. It is dark ambient at its most majestic and lonely.

Place in the Tetralogy: It establishes the spatial and philosophical scale of the cycle. Without this context, the ancient ruins of the following albums would feel less profound. This is the sound of the potential for history.

2. To Athene (July 6, 2024) — The Ruins of Greece

Dedicated to Athena, the goddess of wisdom and warfare, To Athene is the tetralogy's most austere and architectural release. It presents ancient Greece not as a living, breathing civilisation, but as a haunted, abandoned labyrinth.

The album is an archaeological excavation of sound. The title track, "Athene," and others like "Shadow of Focus" and "Underworld" do not celebrate Greek heroism. Instead, they evoke empty marble halls, cold stone corridors, and the ghostly echoes of a vanished people. It is "ruin porn" in audio form, focusing on the melancholy of decay.

The music is dry, mathematically precise, and rhythmically brittle. The IDM structures are sharp and pointillist, with glitches and clicks sounding like crumbling masonry. Field recordings (or their simulations) of wind, distant footsteps, and echoing chambers are foregrounded. There is no warmth here — only the cold, hard truth of entropy.

Place in the Tetralogy: This album grounds the cosmic scale of From Atmos in a physical, decaying reality. It is the sound of history as a wound, not a monument.

3. Egyptology (July 14, 2024) — The Tombs of Egypt

The centerpiece of the tetralogy and arguably the most famous release of qumcl's career, Egyptology received a limited cassette edition through the Kentucky-based label Fantasy Audio Magazine, exposing his music to a wider audience of tape collectors and outsider music enthusiasts.

The album is a deep dive into the funerary rites and pantheon of Ancient Egypt. However, Gerasenko avoids all stereotypical "exotic" musical tropes (no snake charmer melodies or pseudo-Egyptian scales). Instead, each deity-themed track creates a specific, oppressive sonic environment.

Anubis: The god of death. A slow, weighty, ritualistic ambient piece, like being sealed in a sarcophagus.

Ra: The sun god. Built on a dry, hypnotic, low-fidelity techno beat, evoking the relentless, heat-blasted desert.

Sutekh (Set): The god of chaos. The rhythm becomes jagged, aggressive, and unstable, reflecting divine rage.

Thoth: The god of wisdom. A more meditative, looping IDM pattern, suggesting an ancient, calculating intelligence.

Sound: The production is notable for its "sandy" texture — an abundance of micro-noise, crackle, and tape hiss that physically sounds like dust and ancient grit. The bass is deep and oppressive, creating a palpable sense of being underground, in a burial chamber. The album is claustrophobic, mystical, and deeply hypnotic.

Place in the Tetralogy: This is the cycle's emotional and textural heart. It moves from the intellectual distance of To Athene to a more sensory, immersive, and visceral experience. The international cassette release marked the point where the Surgut underground reached a global audience.

4. Olympians (July 26, 2024) — The Power of Olympus

The climactic finale of the tetralogy is a dramatic shift in tone and energy. Where To Athene was cold and Egyptology was oppressive, Olympians is explosive, triumphant, and brutally dynamic.

The album portrays the gods of Olympus not as distant statues but as active, formidable, and dangerous forces of nature. It is the sound of a storm on Mount Olympus, a rave among the ruins. The tracklist directly names the gods (Poseidon, Athene, Aphrodite) and includes a crucial bridge track, "Ancient Egypt," which sonically links the two halves of the tetralogy.

This is the most aggressive and rhythmically driven release of the cycle. The breakbeats are fast, distorted, and relentless. Industrial-tinged techno pulses drive the energy. Synth lines are massive and monolithic, like thunderclaps. Yet, the lo-fi aesthetic remains, preventing the album from sounding "clean" or commercial. It is a glorious, cathartic digital explosion.

Place in the Tetralogy: Olympians provides a dynamic and emotional release after the suffocating atmosphere of Egyptology. It closes the cycle on a note of power, energy, and raw, chaotic celebration. The inclusion of "Ancient Egypt" and a repeated "Athene" theme confirms the four albums are part of a single, integrated musical statement.

"The idea for a mythological tetralogy actually came about quite by chance. I used to play Tomb Raider as a child, and that same year the remasters of the original Tomb Raider games (1–3) were released. Plus, back in the vqopb days, I used samples of Lara Croft from Rise of the Tomb Raider; I've been a fan of this series since I was a child, but it was specifically the first Tomb Raider game that inspired this tetralogy. As you may notice, the first Tomb Raider features locations in Egypt and Ancient Greece, and it also references mythology. Because of this, I began to immerse myself in Ancient Greek mythology and Egyptology; I even have a huge book on the subject. Basically, it all started quite by chance, and I put these releases together very quickly that same month – probably in about 2–4 days, I reckon. But I didn't realise it would give me such a boost."

August--September 2024: Cybernetic Collapse Diptych

The Computer Prime (August 9, qu12): A functioning supercomputer: cold, disciplined, and repetitive. Cyclical IDM rhythms and ambient techno create the atmosphere of retro-futurist server halls.

Your OS Has Been Shut Down (August 23, qu12): The collapse. Rhythms fragment, skip, and self-destruct; tape hiss dominates. The ending moves toward nearly empty ambient space — total system death.

September--October 2024: Deconstruction, Intimacy and Synthesis

Sample Breakdown (September 12, Madcap Laughs): A manifesto of deconstruction: dry, harsh glitch-IDM focused on microscopic levels — short vocal fragments, clicks, broken drum loops, and digital errors.

Trihex (September 2, Qulicker): Hypnotic, monolithic trance and post-industrial techno-ambient built on low-frequency drones, evoking abandoned industrial structures and concrete bunkers. These are unreleased features from the vqopb.

hello mr george demo tapes (September 28, qumcl): A surprising release where IDM crosses with indie rock. Tracks like "dr risperidone" return to themes of mental health, but the sound is sedated. "winter afternoon" and "my cozy house" sound unusually warm, proving Gerasenko's minimalist language can communicate not only paranoia but also nostalgia and emotional fragility.

dsソース (Mixtape) (December 26, qumcl): A grand, retrospective synthesis of the year's aesthetics, blending IDM, vaporwave, ambient, jungle, and lo-fi collage. The remix of Zak Belica's what's the world come to (composer for SIN Episodes) transforms cult game samples into broken, IDM jungle. Raw aggression finally befriended cinematic melancholy.

"To be honest, over the past three years I've hidden and deleted music from the internet many times; once or twice I wanted to completely wipe my SSD and hard drive of all my music and work. There were so many moments like that—there was hatred, envy of others, and aggression. I went through a great deal and it was very bipolar. I do, of course, have schizophrenia, but in fact I have schizoaffective disorder, because this constant bipolar phenomenon affected both my control over the music and its creation, as well as whether to post it online or hide it from people's eyes altogether."

January 2025: PAUSED — A Frozen Moment

After the monumental year-end summary of 2024 with the dsソース mixtape, Gerasenko began the new year with a minimalist but significant statement — the mini-album "PAUSED", released on January 30, 2025.

This release marks not an evolution, but rather a fixation of a state. The title perfectly describes both the sound and the mood of the record.

Soundscape and Atmosphere:

In contrast to the dynamic, event-driven albums of 2024, PAUSED presents a cinematic, frozen snapshot. It is no longer a run through Backrooms corridors or a rave among the ruins of civilization. It is a moment of silence — deep, almost painful meditation.

The musical minimalism refined on albums like I AM ABSOLUTELY SURE... reaches its zenith here. Gerasenko uses the same tools: lo-fi hiss, fragmented IDM rhythms, deep ambient pads, and field recordings. But here, they serve not to create horror, but to convey a feeling of isolation and timelessness.

Tracklist Breakdown:

The album consists of three tracks forming a single, cohesive narrative:

1.    "nowhere code" (04:26): The opening track immediately sets the tone. The title references digital emptiness. The music is built around a looped, aggressive jungle, over which a broken but very restrained rhythm barely pulses. It is the sound of a running algorithm going nowhere.

2.    "happening this morning" (02:58): The shortest and perhaps most fragmented track. The sound resembles a morning awakening captured on damaged tape. Fragments of melodies, rustles, glitches, and distant, muffled beats create an atmosphere of a surreal, sticky morning. The song features an *excerpt from a news report on the 9/11 terrorist attacks*.

3.    "flesh interface" (03:54): The title introduces a disturbing biopunk subtext. This is the most "physical" track on the release, where cold digital structures collide with warmth, an almost organic hum. The techno sounds like a meditation on where the human ends and the machine begins.

Place in the Discography:

"PAUSED" is not a loud manifesto, but an intimate pause. It shows that even in a world made of glitches and noise, there is room for silence and stillness. This album is an important connective tissue, demonstrating that minimalism and melancholy are as crucial pillars of qumcl's work as aggressive breakbeat. It prepares the ground for the more intimate and poetic works of 2025, such as Smoking Delay (Selected Works) and the Fractured Voices project.

February--May 2025: Remasters, Lo-Fi Sketches and the Birth of Fractured Voices

After the minimalist pause of PAUSED in late January 2025, Gerasenko entered a period of reflection, archival curation, and the exploration of new sonic territories.

Trihex (Remastered) — May 26, 2025

Released under his own name, Trihex (Remastered) is not a new album but a significant archival release. The material was recorded approximately between August 2023 and May 2024, originating from the vqopb album era, with some tracks serving as early demos for that collaborative project. This remaster serves as a historical document, cleaning up and presenting these raw, transitional works from the peak of his collaboration with Xiroach. It offers a glimpse into the workshop where the harsh, breakcore-driven sound of that period was forged, now with improved clarity while retaining its characteristic lo-fi grit.

The Light That Bleeds Through Fractured Voices — April 23, 2025

This release marks the launch of Fractured Voices, a new side project and a significant artistic departure. While retaining Gerasenko's signature lo-fi and glitch aesthetics, this project places a much stronger emphasis on spoken word, abstract poetry, and fragmented vocal narratives.

"I think Death Grips inspired me to create Fractured Voices"

The mixtape is a descent into digital paranoia, madness, and psychological decay. Tracks feature repetitive, mantra-like lyrics exploring themes of control ("he is control my mind" ), surveillance ("safety is danger" ), and the erosion of self ("Error brain, no reset, no preset" ). It is a direct continuation of the themes of isolation and mental fragmentation first explored in the Kitchen diptych, but filtered through a new, more lyrical and textural lens.

The music creates an unsettling, liminal space. It blends murky ambient, decaying drone, glitch, and industrial field recordings. The vocals are not sung but whispered, repeated, and distorted, acting as another instrument in the mix.

The final two tracks (10 and 11) feature a collaboration with his frequent British partner Xiroach, adding another layer of glitched-out weirdness to the project's debut.

The entire mixtape functions as a *continuous, 31-minute audio collage*, inviting the listener not to dance, but to get lost inside a fragmented, anxious mind.

Smoking Delay (Selected Works) — May 30, 2025

Just days after the Trihex remaster, Gerasenko released Smoking Delay (Selected Works) under his main qumcl alias. True to its name, it is a collection of

"half-lit sketches and background thoughts, recorded between cigarette breaks and quiet hours."

The artist himself describes it not as a formal album, but as "a dusty folder of ideas that never fully fit elsewhere." This candid framing allows for a more relaxed and intimate listening experience.

The album is a significant stylistic detour. While still lo-fi and hazy, it largely abandons the aggressive beats and complex IDM structures. Instead, the focus is on melancholic guitar-led ambience, raw tape hiss, and soft, poetic electronics.

Original vs. Cover: The material was recorded in two sessions — autumn 2024 and May 2025. It includes 16 tracks, among which is a lo-fi cover of Mac DeMarco's "Chamber Of Reflection" . The rest of the material is original. Tracks like "Just a Rainy Day", "Alone Again", and "With You" feature simple, heartfelt lyrics, marking a rare moment of direct and fragile emotional expression in his discography. The production is raw, unfinished, and deeply personal.

This release is the perfect companion piece to the Fractured Voices project. While Fractured Voices uses words to express digital psychosis, Smoking Delay uses them (and lo-fi melodies) to express a quiet, resigned loneliness. Together, they show Gerasenko moving beyond purely instrumental storytelling into a more song-based, intimate form of expression, setting the stage for the even more ambitious releases of 2026.
