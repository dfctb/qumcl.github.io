Late Summer -- August 2025: Archival Depths, Provocative Statements, and Digital Logic

Following the dark excursion into psychosis with the album An Empty Diagnosis Beyond This Room, August 2025 was marked by a series of releases that can be characterized as an "archival audit" and a "technical manifesto." Egor released a giant retrospective compilation under his old alias, a daring conceptual album, and an album dedicated to pure computer aesthetics.

*Untitled: Resonant Frequency Archive '22--'24* (Madcap Laughs) — August 6, 2025

This *monumental 40-track archive*, released under his first pseudonym Madcap Laughs, is one of the most important archival works in his discography. It acts as a

"spectral transmission from decaying memory,"

capturing the

"unstable transition between analog delirium and digital erasure."

The release is a "forensic reconstruction of sound debris," assembled from *"distorted EPs and salvaged home recordings from 2022-2024."* The title Untitled refers back to the Untitled album of 2023.

The album is a sound collage of tracks that literally "bear the scars of their media": VHS bleeding, circuit-bent instability, and the hum of broken machinery. It includes reworked versions of tracks from earlier albums such as Resonance, Selected Beats, #, Untitled, and BlowFish, as well as completely unreleased material dated 2022. The album's structure is framed by system files: it opens with "Welcome.exe" and closes with "Error.exe."

This archive is not just a collection, but a full-fledged artistic statement, drawing a line under the earlier, rawer period of creativity and making it accessible in one place. "I've polished and finished off the car I built in 2023, back when I was just starting to make my mark in the music scene"

AAEX (Remastered Edition) (Madcap Laughs) — August 17, 2025

This is a remastered edition of the album originally released on June 15, 2024. AAEX dives into the realms of drill 'n' bass, IDM, and lo-fi electronics.

The remaster aims to improve "complex textures and dynamic range," offering a more immersive experience. The album is characterized by fast, fragmented beats, layered soundscapes (glitch, ambient, distortion), and the addition of "analogue warmth" through saturation and tape emulation.

This release demonstrates Gerasenko's commitment to preserving and improving his past catalogue, even for works that are just over a year old.

CONGRATULATIONS! YOU'VE FOUND THE ABSOLUTE WORST ALBUM EVER MADE... (qumcl) — August 19, 2025

This album, released under his main alias qumcl, is a radical and post-ironic gesture. Its title and description are a direct mockery of the listener and, possibly, a response to critics.

The album is positioned as "evidence of failure," "archaeological excavation in the heart of creative bankruptcy," and "a landmark of auditory torture." It is an intentionally provocative statement.

The release consists of 12 tracks, stripped of titles and designated simply as "#1" – "#12." This is an ultra-minimalist approach that forces the listener to focus exclusively on the sound.

The album was recorded from July to September 2024. In terms of sound, it likely represents raw, unedited, and intentionally "bad" material, although the gesture itself is a form of conceptual art, challenging notions of quality and value in music.

"If you remember that album where I told Tunecore to fuck off, the point of this album is the same as that one. Some people said my music was rubbish, the arrangements were rubbish, and generally speaking there was a lot of stuff that wasn't right from a music theory perspective, but to be honest, I didn't give a shit about music theory, but about the people who say that, because I create the rules for music that sounds unusual on the modern scene; the music describes my mental state, and I can't follow life theories any more than they can follow music theory, which generally applies more to classical music. Nerds just want to look clever and superior to everyone else. But you're essentially comparing a palm tree to a birch—they're both trees, but completely different, and found in completely different parts of the world. They also said my logo at the time was a copy of the Aphex Twin, so I decided to put it on the cover."

Digital Arithmetic Logic Units (qumcl) — August 22, 2025

Just three days after the "worst album", Egor releases Digital Arithmetic Logic Units (DALU) — one of his most technically oriented and genre-diverse albums.

Concept: The title refers to computer processor components, emphasizing the "computer materialism" that has been part of his sound since 2024.

Sound: The album is a fusion of Experimental IDM, industrial, ambient, glitch, breakbeat, vaporwave, dubstep, drum n bass, future garage, drone, and lo-fi. It is a true encyclopedia of his electronic influences.

Significance: DALU serves as a bridge between purely analog and digital production methods, exploring "digital textures and rhythmic experiments." It is a continuation of the line begun on releases like The Computer Prime, but here it reaches much greater complexity and scope.

"Xiroach told me it was the best solo album I'd ever released, although to be honest, he thinks my entire discography is brilliant."

Autumn: September-October 2025: Remix Culture and Digital Deconstruction

After the intensive months of archiving, remasters, and technical manifestos, autumn 2025 became a period of more spontaneous creativity, focused on reinterpreting the works of others.

Collection of Weekly Remixes (qumcl) — October 5, 2025

As is clear from the title and description

"A spontaneous batch of remixes made out of boredom. Just October things."

this EP is a spontaneous, almost random collection. It captures a moment of creative freedom where Egor allows himself not to build grand concepts, but simply to experiment with other people's material.

Concept: "A few popular tracks from this week, reimagined through qumcl's hazy electronic lens." It is a look at pop and club culture through his signature distorted, lo-fi filter.

Content: The EP includes 7 tracks. The central track, "Fine Day" (04:36), stands out — it is a deconstruction of the classic anthem "Fine Day" (known from Opus III). The hypnotic, repetitive vocals ("It's a fine day...") are placed in the grip of broken breakbeats, glitches, and a dense ambient atmosphere. Other tracks include "Breakbrotten" (02:42), "Club Bizarre Remix" (03:00), "Club Bizarre Alternative Remix" (02:49), "Vanya Remix" (01:49), "LXN Remix" (04:00), and the short abstract conclusion "iaiaiaiaia" (01:52).

Sound: The collection balances between the genres of electronic, idm, ambient, breakbeat, breakcore, drum n bass, jungle and, of course, lo-fi and weirdcore.

This release is important because it shows Gerasenko's ability to work with recognizable material, turning it into something new, disturbing, and danceable at the same time. It serves as a precursor to the larger remix projects of 2026.

What's the World Come to (qumcl Remix) — October 6, 2025

Released just one day after Collection of Weekly Remixes, this single is an official, full-fledged remix. The track originally belongs to Zak Belica, a composer known for his work on soundtracks for cult games such as SiN Episodes.

Significance: This collaboration is important for several reasons. Firstly, it connects qumcl's music with the world of video games — one of the main foundations of his aesthetic. Secondly, Zak Belica is not just a composer, but a cult figure in the niche of underground game design, adding "geek" credibility to qumcl.

Sound: Unsurprisingly, Egor transforms the original track (likely with elements of industrial rock or electronics) into his signature aggressive, broken IDM/breakcore mix, submerged in lo-fi hiss and glitches.

This release, issued on Xeuqra Records, is an important milestone, demonstrating recognition of his talent by other, albeit niche, but respected authors.

November 2025: The Meta-Archive, Remastered Trauma, and Homages to Rave Classics

November 2025 was the culmination of many trends of the year: archiving, remastering, respect for electronic classics, and a rare, almost euphoric lightness of breath.

U͞n͝t͟i͝t͟l͞e͡d͢_3͟3͠F̵_†ΔΣΣΣ (qumcl) — November 1, 2025

This release is a true monster and, possibly, the most important "meta-archival" project of the year. The title, filled with glitch symbols ("corrupted name"), refers to "extracted VHS Δ archives, frame 33F."

Concept and Scale: The album consists of 40 tracks and represents a total deconstruction of the entire qumcl universe. It is a sound collage where "corrupted" versions of tracks are interspersed with *30-minute drones* and short glitch signals.

Structure as a Map: The tracklist is a direct map of his alter-egos and key works:

*#02 – QUMCLΔ.exe, #03 – qulicker_defrag.sys, #04 – qu12_Σrror, #05 – [madcap-laughs_corrupt.tape]*: Direct references to his pseudonyms.

#08 – xιrσach.c0rrupt, #23 – Xir0@ch, #37 – xiroach♡loves♡you: Homage and collaboration with Xiroach.

*#10, #20, #30, #40 — ENRICH-33E_Nothing*: *Four 30-minute tracks*, referencing the drone project Enrich 33E, representing "pure nothing" and "broadcasting into emptiness."

#18 — EGYPTOLOGY_.mpx, #19 — Ølympians_relic: Corrupted versions of his mythological albums.

#27 — Surgut_mem_frgmnt, #31 — Belarus_Δsync: Fixing his geographical coordinates.

#36 — test sound: The quintessence of the archive, unfinished, draft material.

This album is not for casual listening, but for "audio-archaeology." It draws a line under an entire era, turning his entire discography into a single, deformed, but cyclically closed soundscape.

"I think this album has set a new benchmark in my music; with every album like this, the sound changes, and this happens practically every year or every six months. But it's all down to isolation—I have more time to myself, I want people to know my image as an isolated schizophrenic psychopath, whose flat has its windows boarded up, with no light, and who sits all day making music in the depths of the very cold Siberian wilderness; if ever a Canadian, American or Brit were to find him, they'd simply be in shock."

I Am Absolutely Sure There Are No Corpses In Our Kitchen (Remastered) & I Am Still Absolutely Sure There Are No Corpses In Our Kitchen (Remastered) (Madcap Laughs) — November 1--2, 2025

These two remasters, released under the old pseudonym Madcap Laughs, return the listener to the origins of his psychological sound documentary.

Concept (Part 1): The album, based on personal experience of schizophrenia, blurs the line between perception and delusion. It is an:

"intimate sound diary — a reflection of a mind unfolding and reassembling itself in sound."

The description states:

"This is not a recording about madness — it is the madness itself, echoing through the quiet rooms of reality."

Concept (Part 2): The sequel depicts:

"the mind after collapse — calm, empty, and completely consumed by its own certainty."

This description turns acceptance into horror:

"a mantra of stability whispered from inside the delusion." It is the sound of "loud madness, perfectly still and infinitely repeating."

Both remasters, released on Xeuqra Records, preserve the psychological weight of the originals, adding to them "renewed clarity and precision."

Fine Day (qumcl) — November 12, 2025

After diving into dark archives and psychosis, Egor releases Fine Day — a single that sharply contrasts with the surrounding material.

Concept: This is a full-fledged exploration of the track "Fine Day", which had already appeared as a demo on Collection of Weekly Remixes. Now it receives its own release.

Content: The single includes 4 tracks:

Fine Day (04:36): The original version of the remix.

Fine Day (Alternative Version) (04:36): An alternative mix.

Fine Day (Hard Version) (04:01): A more aggressive, club-oriented version.

Fine Day (Unreleased Version) (04:36): An archival demo version.

Sound: This release is a breath of fresh air. The hypnotic, euphoric vocals of the original classic (Opus III) are placed in his signature broken jungle and drum and bass context. This is, perhaps, the most danceable and "light" track in his discography, proving that the gloomy Siberian producer can also create pure, cathartic joy.

It's Not My Problem (qumcl Remix) & Central Industrial (qumcl Remix) (with The Future Sound of London) — November 20, 2025

Ending the month are two remixes of the legendary British duo The Future Sound of London (FSOL), pioneers of ambient techno and electronic music.

Significance of the collaboration: Working with FSOL is a huge step forward in recognition. FSOL is a cult group that has influenced entire generations of musicians. The fact that Egor remixed them speaks to his growing authority in underground circles.

It's Not My Problem (qumcl Remix): Described as a "reinterpretation in an industrial-techno direction." An aggressive, mechanistic rhythm section and distorted percussion collide with complex, glitchy IDM programming.

Central Industrial (qumcl Remix): Unlike the first, this remix focuses on "ambient techno and atmospheric breakbeats." It preserves FSOL's original industrial soundscapes but restructures them into a deep, meditative, propulsive direction.

These two remixes are the perfect bridge between his respect for the British electronic school (Warp Records, Aphex Twin) and his own unique sound.

December 2025: The Birth of q-rec and the My Schizophrenia Is Worse Remaster

December 2025 was a logical conclusion to the year, summing up the trends of archiving and systematization, and marked an important step in the institutionalization of Egor's work.

The Creation of the q-rec Label

In December 2025 (officially founded in 2026, but the first releases are dated late 2025), Egor launches q-rec — his own label imprint, based in Surgut.

An independent label, acting as a sublabel of Xeuqra Records. This means Xeuqra remains the parent structure, and q-rec is his personal subsidiary.

Created for Russian-speaking musicians, but as Egor noted, he might not run this sublabel for long.

Releases (already planned for December 2025 and 2026):

*X-1 (xiroach) – Crbe (2025)*: The first release on the label, a single by his British associate.

*qumcl – q7_++Z... (2025)*: An album with a long "broken" system hash code in the title, which fully corresponds to his digital decay aesthetic.

Fractured Voices – The Light That Bleeds Through Fractured Voices (Extended) (2026): An extended edition of a key release from his poetic side project.

Faml FX – Roeg Solo Wive Ep (2026) and *Various – q-rec compilation 01 (2026)*: Releases that expand the label's framework and include other artists.

The creation of q-rec is a powerful step, transforming him from just a musician into a curator and owner of his own sonic universe. But besides q-rec, his main creation is Xeuqra.

My Schizophrenia Is Worse (Remastered) (Madcap Laughs) — December 18, 2025

Ending the year is a remaster of the album that was one of the first and most important in his early period. Originally released in May 2023, it documented the transition from pure ambient to more complex IDM structures.

Remaster Description: A "clarified and redefined version of a foundational experimental release." The remaster does not erase the "scars" of the original but makes them "more defined." "Distorted fragments of consciousness, once packed into granular IDM textures, now resonate with new depth."

The album balances between "digital noise and fragmented melodic glimpses." Rhythms "disintegrate and reform." The atmosphere fluctuates "between claustrophobic discomfort and a strangely comforting, nostalgic haze."

This release, along with the remasters of the Kitchen diptych, completes the process of "restoring" the early legacy of Madcap Laughs, making it accessible and restored for new listeners.

**Winter 2026 (January--February): The q-rec Launch, Japanese Psychosis, and a Homage to Aphex Twin

The beginning of 2026 was a direct continuation of the strategic and creative steps outlined at the end of 2025. Egor not only launches his label but also releases the first releases on it, and then shocks the public with two very different, but equally strong albums under his main alias qumcl.

The Light That Bleeds Through Fractured Voices (Extended) (Fractured Voices) — January 2, 2026

The first release of 2026 and one of the first on the new label q-rec* is an extended edition of the debut mixtape of the side project Fractured Voices.

This release develops the ideas of the original 2025 mixtape, adding new material or extending existing tracks. It strengthens the aesthetic of Fractured Voices as a space for "poetry of digital decay," textual paranoia, and industrial ambient.

Releasing this album on q-rec, rather than Xeuqra, emphasizes that side projects and archival material will now be released under Egor's personal control.

*q-rec compilation 01* (Various Artists) — January 11, 2026

Just nine days later, the first official compilation of the label, *q-rec compilation 01*, is released. This is an important step in the institutionalization of the label.

A compilation bringing together 33 tracks from various artists connected to the qumcl ecosystem. Among the participants are qu12 (his alter-ego), It, and others. This demonstrates that q-rec is not just Egor's archive, but a full-fledged platform for "local authors and associates."

*"I originally set up this label to put together a compilation and promote Russian-speaking musicians. The first compilation we released featured my friends from Russia and Xiroach, but to be honest, only 25% of it was written by them; I produced the rest myself and used joint demo tracks with Xiroach."*

The compilation serves as a manifesto of the label, showing its diversity — from IDM and glitch to more experimental and noise-based forms.

気持ち悪い奴 (Kimochi Warui Yatsu / "Disgusting Person") (qumcl) — February 10, 2026

The first solo album of 2026 under the main alias is a return to the darkest, psychological themes, but now expressed through Japanese aesthetics and language.

The album, whose title translates to "Disgusting Person", is an "extremely heavy, explicit, and depressive exploration of psychosis, self-destruction, and self-hatred." It continues the line of his "schizophrenic" releases (Kitchen diptych, An Empty Diagnosis...).

The album consists of 10 tracks, with titles written in Japanese representing painful formulations:

*脳が俺を罵る (Nou ga Ore wo Nonosiru / "Brain curses me") (04:51)*

*分裂した独り言 (Bunretsu sita Hitorigoto / "Split monologue / Schizophrenic self-talk") (03:33)*

*大便 (Daiben / "Shit") (03:59)*

*自殺 (Jisatsu / "Suicide") (04:25)*

*へんたい (Hentai / "Pervert" or "Anomaly") (05:32)*

*役立たず (Yakutatazu / "Useless") (02:56)*

*クソ野郎 (Kuso Yarou / "Bastard" / "Motherfucker") (05:30)*

*誰もいない声 (Daremo Inai Koe / "Voice from nowhere / Nobody's voice") (01:01)*

*ずないざつおн (Zunai Zatsuon / "Intracranial noise") (02:46)*

*気持ち悪い奴 (Kimochi Warui Yatsu / "Disgusting Person") (04:21)*

The music is on the edge of breakcore, hardcore, raw dance, and noise. Beautiful ambient passages are ruthlessly torn apart by machine-gun bursts of breakcore and distorted noise. It is the sound of a mind shattering into pieces.

Japanese Context: The use of the Japanese language adds a layer of "foreignness" and detachment, allowing him to speak on extremely personal topics through the lens of Eastern aesthetics, referencing cult psychological anime and Japanese weirdcore.

"I've really wanted to do something really raw ever since 2023"

COME TO DADDY — SIGNAL ABUSE (qumcl & Aphex Twin) — February 22, 2026

The culmination of February and, possibly, one of the most ambitious and conceptually important releases in Egor's career.

This is an unofficial tribute remix of the legendary Aphex Twin EP Come to Daddy (1997, Warp Records). The title "SIGNAL ABUSE" perfectly describes Egor's approach: he takes the original breakcore manifesto and subjects it to "signal abuse."

The music simulates an "analog hardware explosion." Aphex's original vocal samples ("I will eat your soul") and drum machines are run through a broken mixing console, burnt magnetic tape, and a corrupted hard drive.

The album is structured as 7 "studies" :

Study I (The year is 1997) (01:49)

Study II (Main Reconstruction) (04:26)

Study III (Alternate Cut) (05:28)

Study IV (Distortion Layer) (04:26)

Study V (Break Study) (04:26)

Study VI (Structural Error) (05:21)

Study VII (Harsh Noise) (01:11)

This is not just a remix, but a dialogue with an idol. Egor proves that he is not a blind imitator, but an artist capable of reinterpreting and destroying the original, creating something new and uncompromising.

March 2026: Source Engine Dreams, System Collapse, and Tribute to the Greats

March 2026 continued the trend of recent months: active collaboration with Xiroach, immersion in cyber and gaming aesthetics, and bold, conceptually refined remixes of artists key to Egor.

Nzxjiy (qumcl & Xiroach) — March 15, 2026

This collaborative album with his British associate Xiroach is a direct continuation of their sound dialogue begun in 2024 with vqopb, of which it is a continuation.

The album consists of 26 tracks and is a true celebration for geeks and gamers. The track titles are encrypted in "leetspeak" (using numbers instead of letters) and are direct references to the game universes of Half-Life, Portal, and also to Aphex Twin.

Tracklist as a Map of References:

*H4lf-L1f3_L04d1ng_Scr33n..., H4lf-L1f3_2_L04d1ng_5cr33ns..., N1ght_Bu5_Fr0m_C1ty_17...*: Direct homages to the world of Half-Life and City 17.

P0rt4l_Turrets_S1ng1ng_Lull4b13s..., Th3_L45t_Tr41n_Fr0m_4p3rtur3_5c13nc3...: References to the Portal universe and Aperture Science.

Dr1ft1ng_Thr0ugh_X3n_W1th_4ph3x_Tw1n_Wh1sp3r1ng...: A direct address to his idol, combining drift through the interdimensional world of Xen (Half-Life) with the "whisper" of Aphex Twin.

Unlike aggressive hardcore, Nzxjiy sounds like a cinematic lo-fi sound collage.

"Torn rhythmic jungle patterns and glitch effects are submerged in an icy, industrial dark ambient,"

creating an atmosphere of abandoned laboratories, empty corridors, and rusted servers.

Seven Minutes Before the System Forgot Everything (Fractured Voices) — March 19, 2026

This release under the side project Fractured Voices is a direct continuation of the "digital decay" aesthetic and is, in essence, a *10-track conceptual EP*.

The title and tracklist are built around the idea of a critical system failure, loss of identity, and digital amnesia. It is a sound portrait of the moment "seven minutes before the system forgot everything."

The EP consists of short, fragmented tracks, ranging from *12-second error signals* to *almost 5-minute IDM constructions*.

Boot Sequence Failed (00:41), Echoes Looping In A Dead Channel (00:12), Error Code: Identity Not Found (04:49): Key tracks creating a narrative about technical and mental degradation.

It also includes two remixes ("Dead Link And Thoughts Derailed") of tracks from his own catalog, adding self-reference and closure to the release.

Pulsewidth (qumcl Remix) & Resonance (qumcl Remix) — March 23, 2026

The culmination of March was the release of two remixes of the most important artists for Egor.

Pulsewidth (qumcl Remix):

This is a remix of a gem from *Selected Ambient Works 85-92* — the album which, by Egor's own admission, is "the foundation of everything I do now."

Unlike the aggressive deconstruction of Come to Daddy, this remix

"immerses the bright, nostalgic melody in a dirty environment: the rhythm of the original analog techno is overloaded, broken by micro-glitches, and enveloped in a dense layer of Surgut's cassette lo-fi noise."

It is a tribute, not a destruction.

Resonance (qumcl Remix) (with HOME):

The second remix is of the cult synthwave hit "Resonance" by American producer HOME. This track, with hundreds of millions of streams, is known for its warm, nostalgic, and sunny atmosphere. Egor performs a radical stylistic trick: he "transplants this sunny Californian melody onto cold Siberian soil." The composition is immersed in his signature dirty lo-fi environment, becoming overgrown with IDM structures and cassette noise.

Both remixes, released on the same day, demonstrate the dichotomy of his approach. With one idol (Aphex Twin), he engages in a dialogue, deforming his sound. With the other (HOME), he performs an act of cultural transplantation, forever changing its "weather." This double release is the perfect manifesto of the "Surgut sound," reinterpreting global electronic classics and contemporary music.

Present Tense (April--May 2026): The Manifesto, The Archive, The Remix, and The Live Test

April and May 2026 became the brightest and most significant point in Egor's discography. Within a few weeks, he released his most important works to date, drawing a line under the past and establishing himself as a mature, uncompromising author ready for new forms.

WITHOUT MUSIC, LIFE WOULD BE A MISTAKE (Fractured Voices) — April 27, 2026

This album, released under the most poetic pseudonym Fractured Voices, is one of the most mature and lyrical statements of his career. The title — a quote from Friedrich Nietzsche — sounds like a credo.

The album is dedicated to themes of digital degradation, memory failure, and emptiness. The track titles are structured as fragments of system logs and errors: "bitrate of a mistake", "silence.exe has stopped working", "data decay for memory", "distortion of a vanished melody".

The album consists of 10 tracks, creating a unified narrative about technological and spiritual amnesia.

The music gently balances between melancholic ambient and glitch. It is not aggressive noise, but rather a "personal audio diary that has partially been erased and damaged by time." Of particular note is track 8, "Pulse Without Frequency", which is a tribute/remix to the work of Flying Lotus, adding another cult experimentalist to his palette of influences.

i uploaded 95% of my unreleased stuff (qumcl) — April 28, 2026

Just one day after the poetic album, Egor performs, perhaps, the most radical gesture of his career. This release is a colossal archive of 581 tracks, united under the collective title "Unknown."

This is a total hard drive wipe. "I uploaded 95% of my unreleased stuff." An act of absolute anti-commercial punk, digital Dadaism, and creative freedom.

581 tracks, stripped of titles. The duration ranges from *2-second glitches* to *17-minute ambient drones* (track #59). It is digital chaos, a dump of consciousness and archive simultaneously.

This gesture draws a line under his past. He is no longer burdened by unreleased material. It is a challenge to the listener ("Can you handle this chaos?" ) and, at the same time, a liberation for the author himself. Alongside the Kitchen diptych, this is the most important conceptual release of his career.

Not Gonna Get Us (qumcl Remix) (with t.A.T.u.) — May 13, 2026

Following the archive, Egor releases, perhaps, the loudest and most public remix of his career — of the cult hit by the duo t.A.T.u. "Not Gonna Get Us".

Egor takes the recognizable, fast-paced vocal parts and completely destroys the original Eurodance foundation. The track turns into a "mechanical, paranoid, and harsh electronic action film."

This is hardcore jungle and dirty industrial techno. High-speed, machine-gun drum breaks collide with a heavy techno pulse, sharp noise attacks, and micro-glitches. It is a remix-deconstruction, turning nostalgia for the 2000s into a disturbing, urban rave.

*qumcl live test 12/05/2026* (qumcl) — May 14, 2026

The culmination of this period becomes a recording of a live performance. "Live Test" is a *48-minute set*, divided into two improvisational parts.

This is a direct fixation of how his music sounds in real time, without studio post-production. It is a statement that he is ready for concert activity.

Structure:

Part 1 (31:30): A dynamic, large-scale part where deep IDM and cosmic jungle escalate into machine-gun breakcore, a dense hardcore rhythm, and dirty distortion. It is within this part that tracks by Aphex Twin mixed with Egor's own tracks are heard. As he himself noted: "I bought DJ equipment then and was checking how it sounds, I didn't have a ready-made set, I just played what was on my ThinkPad"

Part 2 (17:00): The concluding, more meditative part. It moves away from breakbeat into deep, isolated dark ambient and weirdcore sound fragments, simulating the "cooling down of the equipment."

The release captures the artist at his peak form: free from the archive, technically flawless, and ready for large-scale live performances.

Backrooms (qumcl) — May 17, 2026

Released just three days after the concert test, this release is a direct sonic immersion into the aesthetic of the internet phenomenon of the same name.

«Backrooms» is a well-known creepypasta and visual meme, describing endless, empty, yellowish office labyrinths filled only with the hum of fluorescent lights and a feeling of total, oppressive isolation. It is the quintessence of "liminal space" — a transitional place you are trapped in forever.

It fully corresponds to the Backrooms culture. It is claustrophobic drone ambient and glitch noise. The music does not develop; it is static, monotonous, and endless. Humming, low-frequency vibration, rare but sharp sound artifacts create the perfect, unbearable soundtrack to the loss of sanity in endless subconscious corridors.

This release shows that even after the powerful purging gesture (the archive of 581 tracks) and the statement of intent for concert activity, Egor remains true to his root aesthetic — creating sound worlds that convey loneliness, anxiety, and digital terror. As of now (May 2026), «Backrooms» is the logical conclusion of themes begun in the Kitchen diptych and An Empty Diagnosis Beyond This Room, but transferred to the collective digital unconscious. Will there be a continuation? We do not know. But the theme of mental illness is very important in Egor's discography.

Studio Architecture and Gear: The Tools Behind the Sound (2024 Era)

The hardware and software setup of Egor Gerasenko in 2024 plays a critically important role in understanding his musical evolution. It is through the combination of vintage electronics, modern IDM tools, cassette degradation, and powerful digital processing that the signature qumcl sound — a blend of cinematic ambient, weirdcore, lo-fi IDM, industrial techno, and post-internet melancholy — was forged.

The year 2024 was the moment when a home underground setup transformed into a full-fledged experimental electronic studio, capable of producing a wide spectrum of sounds:

Psychotic dark ambient (I AM STILL ABSOLUTELY SURE THERE ARE NO CORPSES IN OUR KITCHEN);

Complex breakbeat-IDM (vqopb);

Mythological cinematic ambient (Egyptology, Olympians);

Retro-futuristic vaporwave/weirdcore (dsソース);

Intimate lo-fi diary ambient (hello mr george demo tapes).

Central Computer System

The studio's foundation is built around two primary machines: an Apple MacBook Pro Late 2011 and a Lenovo ThinkPad T480s.

The old MacBook Pro perfectly fits the qumcl aesthetic, possessing the characteristic "digital warmth" of older audio drivers, making it ideal for live looping, ambient production, cassette workflows, and glitch sequencing. This machine likely participated in the early weirdcore period, the Kitchen diptych, ambient sketches, demo tape workflows, and cassette bounce processing. Aesthetically, it aligns with the spirit of liminal internet, old web, 2010s vaporwave, and decayed digital culture.

The primary production machine, however, is a modern high-end electronic production workstation: a Ryzen 5 5600X with an NVIDIA RTX 5060 and 32 GB of DDR4 RAM. This upgrade explains the sharp increase in scale, clarity, and technical complexity in the 2024 sound — particularly audible on albums like vqopb, Olympians, Trihex, The Computer Prime, and dsソース.

Central DAW Ecosystem

The core of his production is Ableton Live 12 Suite (and previously Live 11) paired with an Ableton Push 2. Ableton is ideal for qumcl's philosophy: loop-based composition, glitch mutation, IDM sequencing, breakbeat fragmentation, and live ambient layering. The Push 2 is especially important, as it turns music into a near-physical performance, allowing him to launch scenes, break rhythms in real-time, restructure loops on the fly, and create hypnotic repetitive structures. This explains the cyclical trance of Trihex, the disintegrating rhythms of Sample Breakdown, the long acid progressions of vqopb, and the hypnotic drones of From Atmos.

Cassette Architecture and Tape Degradation

The symbolic heart of the entire studio is the JVC TD W118 (and a TASCAM 202MKV) cassette deck. It is the source of much of qumcl's lo-fi aesthetic: tape hiss, wow and flutter, degraded ambience, VHS warmth, dusty saturation, and cassette artifacts. These hardware units are supplemented by a suite of software emulations: Wavesfactory Cassette, Aberrant DSP SketchCassette II, Softube Tape, Jonas Eriksson Tape Fiasco, and *XLN Audio RC-20 Retro Color*. This part of the setup is responsible for the feeling of old VHS tapes, liminal nostalgia, home-recorded melancholy textures, and degraded memory aesthetics — essential to albums like hello mr george demo tapes, the Kitchen diptych, dsソース, To Athene, and Egyptology.

Roland and the Classic IDM/Techno Foundation

Gerasenko's setup is surprisingly close to classic IDM/techno studios of the late 1990s. Key devices in his arsenal include the iconic *Roland TB-303*, *TR-808*, *TR-909*, *TR-606*, *TR-707*, *TR-727*, *SH-101*, *Juno-60*, *Juno-106*, *Jupiter-8*, *JD-800*, and *D-50*. This architecture forms the basis of his acid basslines, industrial drums, cold digital pads, old-school techno atmosphere, and rave-era percussion.

D16 Group: The Acid and Industrial Engine

An important part of the setup is the D16 Group suite, including Phoscyon 2, Drumazon 2, Nepheton 2, Nithonat 2, Devastor 2, and Decimort 2. These are the foundation of his acid techno, harsh IDM, distorted breakbeat, and industrial rave aesthetics, forming the sonic philosophy behind vqopb, Olympians, and The Computer Prime.

Glitch and Digital Destruction

Key tools for digital destruction are dBlue (Illformed) Glitch 2, Cableguys ShaperBox 3 Bundle, Devious Machines Infiltrator 2, and Potenza DSP Amigo Sampler. They explain the rhythmic collapse, broken sequencing, sample mutation, digital decay, and buffer-style glitches found in the disintegrating beats of Sample Breakdown, the digital agony of Your OS Has Been Shut Down, and the psychotic noise fragmentation of the Kitchen diptych.

Cinematic Ambient and Sci-Fi Layer

The primary cinematic instruments are Spectrasonics Omnisphere, Native Instruments Kontakt 5, Valhalla VintageVerb, and Valhalla Plate, alongside a vast park of Arturia emulations: the *CS-80*, Prophet, DX7, Synclavier, Mellotron, Buchla, Emulator II, *Jup-8*, Synthi, and *Matrix-12*. This is where his sci-fi melancholy, liminal ambience, retro-futuristic atmosphere, and cinematic drone architecture are born, particularly heard on From Atmos, Egyptology, To Athene, dsソース, and the track "another resonance prospector".

Live Instruments and the "Home-Recorded" Layer

Despite the dominance of electronics, his setup also includes live instruments: a *Jet Guitars JS-300*, a Yamaha TRBX174 Bass, and a Yamaha PSR E373. This explains the indie rock fragments, soft-rock melancholy, and home-demo aesthetics present on hello mr george demo tapes, particularly the tracks "lovely lasagna song" and "my cozy house".

Monitoring and Studio Environment

The studio chain is completed by a Focusrite Scarlett 2i2 interface, PreSonus Eris E5 XT studio monitors, and *Audio-Technica ATH-M40x* headphones, a configuration ideal for close-up IDM mixing, detailed noise processing, ambient depth balancing, and cassette texture monitoring.

Conclusion

The qumcl 2024 setup is a unique hybrid of old internet culture, 1990s IDM, industrial techno, weirdcore aesthetics, cassette nostalgia, and cinematic ambient production. Gerasenko's greatest achievement as a producer is that he does not use equipment for the sake of "vintage" coolness. All the technology is integrated into the artistic concept: cassettes become memory, glitch becomes mental decay, ambient becomes liminal space, acid and techno become digital anxiety, and old synthesizers become the voices of broken electronics.

This is why the releases of 2024 do not sound like a collection of genre experiments, but as a single, vast universe of post-Soviet digital surrealism.
