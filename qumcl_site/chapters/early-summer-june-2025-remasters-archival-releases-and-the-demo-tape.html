Early summer-June 2025: Remasters, Archival Releases, and the Demo Tape

Following the introspective releases of spring 2025 (the Trihex remaster, Smoking Delay, and the launch of Fractured Voices), Gerasenko dedicated June to revisiting, refining, and systematically archiving his recent past. This month saw the release of two significant remasters and a monumental demo compilation.

Olympians (Remastered) — June 14, 2025

Originally released in July 2024 as the climactic finale of his mythological cycle, Olympians received a comprehensive remaster. While the original was characterised by a deliberately raw, diary-like lo-fi aesthetic, this new version brought

"a new sense of depth and clarity — while preserving the warmth and intimacy of the original."

The album remains an instrumental exploration of Greek mythology, painting sonic portraits of archetypes: from the god of thunder to the goddess of wisdom, from oceanic depths to the sands of Ancient Egypt. The remaster allows the cinematic themes and melancholic introspection to breathe more freely, making it an ideal soundtrack for

"nighttime walks, reflection, daydreams, or simply drifting away."

The cover art features Artemisia Gentileschi's Penitent Magdalene (c. 1625--30), reinforcing the album's themes of introspection and mythological melancholy.

PAUSED (Remastered) — June 15, 2025

Just one day later, Gerasenko released a remastered version of his January 2025 mini-album, PAUSED. The original release was a minimalist, three-track meditation on stasis and isolation. The remaster enhances its core concept, described by the artist as blending "lo-fi drum and bass with minimal techno textures and a dusty, tape-saturated warmth."

The remaster likely brings greater clarity to its fragmented rhythms ("nowhere code" ), muffled soundscapes ("happening this morning" ), and organic-digital hybrid textures ("flesh interface" ), without sacrificing the original's hazy, nostalgic atmosphere.

*Demo Tape 2023-2024* — June 16, 2025

The most significant archival release of the month, *Demo Tape 2023-2024*, is a collection of 21 electronic sketches and personal experiments recorded between 2023 and 2024. True to its name, it functions as a "dusty folder" of raw ideas, capturing the artist's creative process in its most unpolished form.

The release is notable for several reasons:

Physical Edition: A limited run of original demo tapes was released as cassettes, which quickly sold out, demonstrating a dedicated collector base.

Tracklist as Archive: The track titles are often direct descriptors, such as "72 IDM", "74 Old Chill Ambient Techno", "82 Sounds like EDM Techno", "85 Tomb Raider 1", "92 Tomb Raider 2", "103 Vaporwave Vibes", and "108 Energy Dnb". This candid labelling offers a fascinating glimpse into Gerasenko's creative workflow and his diverse range of influences.

Lo-fi Ethos: The collection is not about polished production, but about "memory fragments, emotional noise, and private moments caught on tape." It features lo-fi textures, nostalgic synths, broken rhythms, and ghostly ambiences, all recorded on a home setup with field recordings.

The Demo Tape serves as a perfect companion piece to the spring's Smoking Delay (Selected Works), offering an even rawer, less-filtered look into the artist's sonic sketchbook and solidifying the first half of 2025 as a period dedicated to introspection and archiving.

Mid-Summer July 2025: Egyptology Remastered, the WERNER Single, and a Descent into Psychosis

Following the archival work of June, Gerasenko continued to refine his back catalogue in July, while also releasing a standalone single and a harrowing new full-length album that marked a stark return to the themes of mental illness.

Egyptology (Remastered) — July 13, 2025

A little over a year after its original release, Gerasenko's breakthrough mythological album received a comprehensive remaster. The original *6-track album (XQ0008)* was expanded into a *17-track archive*. The remaster includes all original tracks ("Anubis", "Ra", "Sutekh", "Thoth", "Horus", "Osiris") alongside a wealth of demo material. These demos offer a fascinating insight into the album's development, featuring early versions of tracks like "Isis (Demo)", "Khonsu (Demo)", and "Shu (Demo)". Notably, it also includes "Khonsu (Xiroach Version) (Demo)", showcasing the continued collaboration with his British partner. Additional demos like "Shadow of Focus (Demo)", "Anniversary (Demo)", and "Underworld (Demo)" further expand this release into a comprehensive document of the Egyptology creative sessions.

WERNER — July 14, 2025

Released as a standalone digital single just one day after the Egyptology remaster, "WERNER" is a track that had previously appeared as a bonus track on the Fantasy Audio Magazine cassette version of Egyptology. Its release as a single suggests it holds a particular significance for Gerasenko. The track is characterised by its harsh, industrial, and rhythmic nature, fitting squarely within his IDM and breakcore leanings. It serves as a potent reminder of his harder-edged production style, contrasting with the more ambient and demo-oriented material being released around the same time.

An Empty Diagnosis Beyond This Room — July 26, 2025

The most significant release of the month, and one of the most challenging in his entire discography, is An Empty Diagnosis Beyond This Room. Described by the artist as

"the first phase of an auditory dissection of uncontrolled schizophrenia psychosis"

this album is a direct and disturbing return to the themes first explored in the 2023--2024 Kitchen diptych.

"I was sketching out ideas for this album; I remember walking around my neighbourhood and taking photos of interesting places that would fit the album's concept, because specifically those places I photographed, and indeed the whole neighbourhood in general, I associated with my childhood, and following acute psychotic episodes I have brain damage which is why I constantly dream about these places, this neighbourhood; I also made notes on my phone and in a notebook. Initially I wanted to take photos of a psychiatric hospital but I wouldn't have been able to because of restricted access, and besides, the ward itself looked like a normal hospital from the outside, but in reality, inside it was a prison with wards; in some wards there was a mixed ward of people, in others there was a ward for severely ill alcoholics, severely ill schizophrenics and the mentally disabled, and people with dementia. I made this album specifically about that period, but to be honest, I was lying there quite comfortably on my own and didn't have any difficult situations with the orderlies."

The album is structured as a seven-track descent into madness, documenting

"the collapse of rational soundscapes into a vortex of radio interference, VHS decay, and harsh noise."

Concept: The work is framed as "a direct transmission from the edge of audible reality", recorded in domestic isolation using "fragmented field recordings, malfunctioning electronics, and repurposed media artifacts."

Track Breakdown: Each track represents a distinct phase of the psychotic episode.

*P1.1 - Admission to Nowhere (8:35)*: The threshold of psychosis cracks open.

*P1.2 - Schizophonic Waltz (4:21)*: A distorted dance with intrusive voices.

*P1.3 - The Sadness of a Broken Gramophone (5:12)*: Melodies disintegrate into mechanical sobs.

*P1.4 - Lobotomy of Memory (16:20)*: Surgical static erases recall. This is the album's longest and most harrowing piece.

*P1.5 - Dementia Praecox Hymns (5:53)*: Choral fragments from a crumbling mind.

*P1.6 - Emergency Broadcast (17:01)*: Pirate signals from a collapsing reality.

*P1.7 - Parasitic Echoes of a Prayer (22:48)*: Final possession of the stereo field.

Sound: Musically, the album exists at the "intersection of experimental music, sound art, and involuntary documentation." It largely abandons traditional rhythm and melody in favour of oppressive dark ambient, harsh noise, glitch, and unsettling drone.

"I was mainly very inspired by the Caretaker project, but as you know, there are loads of people online doing similar parodies, or perhaps I just find the theme of dementia and his albums—such as Everywhere at the End of Time—to be truly experimental and unusual; but essentially these albums are simply an interpretation of dementia; whilst he documents the condition based on a relative suffering from it, I approached it here through my own experience, drawing on the sensations of the illness and having been hospitalised three times in a single year at the height of my illness. The album is very good, but not everyone will understand it properly; personally, I wouldn't want my album to go viral on TikTok in the same way as Caretaker's album, because people definitely won't grasp the theme of schizophrenic disorders; it's not for most people, because most people don't understand what a mental illness entails, let alone a severe mental illness such as a schizophrenic disorder. But this is only the first part of the album; to be honest, I don't want to release the next parts because it would just be another Caretaker knock-off, although that's not the case – I created and mixed all the sounds here myself, rather than just buying records from a dump and making an album out of them. They're different things; it's just that the concept of the albums is similar."

This release is a powerful and uncomfortable listen. It makes no concessions to the listener, instead forcing them to inhabit the artist's fractured mental state. It stands as a testament to Gerasenko's willingness to use his music as a raw, unfiltered medium for processing and documenting his most personal and painful experiences. After a spring and early summer focused on remasters, demos, and introspective lo-fi sketches, An Empty Diagnosis serves as a brutal reminder of the turmoil that underpins much of his artistic output.
